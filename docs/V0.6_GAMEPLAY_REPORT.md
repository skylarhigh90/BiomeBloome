# Biome Bloome V0.6 gameplay progression report

## Outcome

V0.6 implements the five high-priority findings from the gameplay audit without adding species or terrain breadth. The work concentrates on agency, readable causality, attachment to individual animals, fair recovery from mistakes, and a more reliable reward cadence.

The resulting deliberate seeded playthrough completes in **522.3 simulation seconds**, above the intended 500-second pacing floor. Its post-opening act waits are approximately **75, 96, 101, 99, and 138 seconds**. The same run produces **40 natural births, 5 successful hunts, 4 starvation losses, 20 surviving Rabbits, and 2 surviving Foxes**. This is a healthier curve than the previous quick-completion path: each act has time to develop, but no mid-run proof creates a multi-hundred-second near-miss loop.

## The six acts

| # | Act | New decision or outcome | Reward |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | The First Meal | Place four Rabbits and help three different founders eat | Berry Bush unlock, two bushes, terrain reveal |
| 2 | The First Family | Two births; one young Rabbit grows and eats | Transplant unlock, one charge, terrain reveal |
| 3 | Two Lasting Homes | Two separated nurseries plus productive Carrot and Berry sites | One Transplant charge, terrain reveal |
| 4 | A Nursery Network | Carry the two homes forward and establish a third | Fox unlock, two Foxes, Web supplies, terrain reveal |
| 5 | Hunt and Recover | Two hunts, two births, two living hunters, and Rabbit population recovery | Living supplies, one Transplant charge, larger terrain reveal |
| 6 | Living Balance | Three nurseries and healthy prey conditions while three births and three hunts coexist in one recent window | Completion and sandbox epilogue |

Forced hunt/birth order was removed. The predator acts now judge what the ecosystem achieved, not whether autonomous animals happened to produce events in a prescribed sequence. The final 150-second rolling window preserves difficulty and freshness without requiring a lucky order.

Repeated proofs were also removed. Only The First Family asks a newborn to grow and eat. Existing homes carry into the nursery-network act. The final act deliberately recombines prior systems, but it does so as one simultaneous whole-ecosystem outcome rather than resetting the same isolated counters.

## Player agency and legibility

### Placement quality

The cursor now reports **Rich**, **Fair**, **Poor**, or **Invalid** before placement. Plants disclose the terrain-capacity percentage and preferred terrain; Rabbits report reachable forage; Foxes report reachable prey and the projected prey-per-Fox ratio. The world-space ring uses matching semantic colors.

### Undo and Transplant

The latest placement can be undone for five real seconds, returning the exact item to inventory. The window expires during pause as well as play, so its meaning is predictable.

Transplant is an earned, limited correction tool rather than unrestricted rearrangement. The player selects an existing plant and a new site, spends one charge, and retains 65% of current biomass subject to the new site's capacity. Charges arrive after the acts where terrain understanding or predator recovery matters.

### Individual animal stories

Every Rabbit and Fox has a deterministic name and public snapshot: life stage, hunger state, activity, parents, number of young, meals or hunts, and latest life event. Natural reproduction records both parents and links the child back to each parent. Clicking an animal opens its field note, while Follow smoothly tracks it until the player pans, zooms, closes the note, or toggles Follow off.

The three-line **Meadow Moments** feed surfaces named arrivals, births, first meals, hunts, transplants, and undos. It converts simulation changes into small stories without exposing debug-only evaluator data.

## Reward and sound cadence

Every completed act now produces a persistent terrain reveal plus an unlock, inventory introduction, tool charge, supply-pool change, or completion. Objective-card pulses and minor/major/final toast treatments remain distinct.

`game/meadow_audio_director.gd` synthesizes all audio at runtime, so the build has no third-party audio dependency. A soft looping meadow bed supports separate cues for animal/plant placement, birth, first meal, hunt, transplant, supply arrival/claim, milestone, danger, recovery, and final completion. The top-bar audio control mutes only this game-owned sound layer.

## Playtest-led corrections

The first deliberate rerun revealed that a stricter third-nursery separation plus a long confirmation timer could repeatedly reach 99% and reset as autonomous Rabbits wandered. The strategic requirement was sound, but the wait was not. The network now keeps the already-taught 240-unit separation and uses an 8-second confirmation. A deliberate bot that actually spends saved/supply Rabbits on the requested third nursery reaches the predator act around 285 seconds instead of waiting more than 450 seconds on incidental movement.

The final act initially repeated that problem with a long hold layered on top of a 150-second rolling ecology window. The window already proves persistence, so the redundant confirmation is four seconds. Its substantive requirements remain unchanged.

A second late-game test found a soft random gate when an older Fox died and no replacement was in inventory. Meadow Mail now guarantees that one of its choices contains a Fox whenever Foxes are unlocked, fewer than two are alive, and none is already in the satchel. The player still chooses and places the replacement; progression no longer waits solely on bundle RNG.

Strategy contrast remains meaningful:

- Dense dumping reaches the first two acts but stalls at **Two Lasting Homes** with only one nursery. Quantity cannot bypass geography.
- Deliberate three-center play completes all six acts at **522.3 seconds** and can continue into sandbox observation.
- A checkpoint-reactive 24-Rabbit stress run reaches **Living Balance** by 474 seconds and remains viable at 900 seconds, but its overpopulation causes 15 starvation losses and unstable nursery evidence. It demonstrates recoverable ecological consequences rather than a collapse or an automatic win.
- Moderate predator overstock (a third Fox) remains recoverable but delays completion to **535.7 seconds** and pushes the prey ratio close to its limit. The separate extreme eight-Fox ecology scenario still collapses a 14-Rabbit population to zero and leaves four surviving Foxes. This gives the warning meaningful teeth without turning one extra placement into an invisible hard failure.

## Why no new animals or terrain yet

New species and terrain would add breadth but would not directly solve the audit's strongest problems: hidden causality, repeated proofs, event-order frustration, irreversible mistakes, weak attachment, and sparse feedback. V0.6 makes the existing Rabbits, Foxes, plants, Meadow, Woodland, Thicket, and Stream more expressive first.

The next content expansion should wait for external player data. Useful signals include act completion rates, time spent with field notes/following, undo and Transplant usage, the goal responsible for final-act stalls, mute rate, and whether players can explain why a placement is rich or poor. If those validate the current loop, one new species should then introduce a genuinely new relationship rather than another population counter.

## Validation

- Focused progression/interaction suite: 40 tests, including outcome order, rolling-window expiry, identity, lineage, the clickable field note/follow action, feed, placement quality, undo, Transplant, audio, supplies, rewards, Critical recovery, HUD mapping, and full completion.
- Core behavior suite: 41 tests.
- Terrain generation, reachability, and route-budget suite: 13 tests.
- Plant ecology scenarios: sustainable grazing, overgrazing, redistribution, recovery, and no-alternative starvation all pass.
- Five ecological scenario comparisons pass.
- Rendered 1280×800 inspection confirms the new right rail, field note, action strip, top controls, and existing objective/world hierarchy do not overlap.
